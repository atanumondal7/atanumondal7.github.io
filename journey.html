Journey

From logic gates to a UVM environment, one level of abstraction at a time.

  1. 1

    Circuits and logic

    I started with circuit analysis, semiconductor physics and digital logic. In Logisim I built a 4-bit ALU, a JK flip-flop, a 4-bit subtractor and a lottery machine, and I modeled an NMOS common-source amplifier in Falstad.

    • Logisim
    • Falstad
    • Tinkercad
  2. 2

    Verilog, from gates up

    I rebuilt the same ideas in Verilog at different levels: gate-level, behavioral, and with randomization. An ALU, an accumulator, a ripple-carry adder, a JK flip-flop, a 4-to-1 mux and a traffic light.

    • Verilog
    • EDA Playground
  3. 3

    SystemVerilog and class-based testbenches

    I moved to SystemVerilog classes: generators, drivers, monitors and scoreboards. The bugs taught me the most, such as clocking-block skew, races between monitor and driver, and setup, hold and metastability worked out from first principles.

    • SystemVerilog
    • Questa
  4. 4

    Coverage-driven verification

    Constrained-random stimulus, cross coverage and self-checking scoreboards. The 4-bit ALU testbench reached 100% functional coverage, and the universal shift register testbench tracks 85 coverage bins.

    ALU testbenchShift register testbench

  5. 5

    UVM

    I built a UVM environment for a UART in loopback, which passed 1000 of 1000 transactions, and earned Siemens' SystemVerilog UVM Comprehensive Pathway badge by passing three exams.

    UART projectUVM badges

  6. now

    What I'm building now

    I2C building blocks, starting with a shift register and an open-drain bus pad, and finishing the digital alarm clock.
